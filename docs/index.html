An AI agent that triages incidents, finds repeat failures and quantifies operational loss

The agent reads each new IT incident ticket, suggests its severity and owning team, links it to similar past incidents and routes it to the right level of human review. It never closes a ticket or changes severity on its own.

      Step 1 · Business decision and risk outcome
      
    Every incident needs three fast decisions, and getting them wrong costs money
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    A mid-sized bank's service desk logs about  IT incidents a month across payments, card authorization, digital banking and back-office systems. For each one, a triage coordinator decides:
    
        
      
          
    • How severe is it? Severity sets the response clock, from 4 hours for SEV-1 to 72 hours for SEV-4.
      • 
          
    • Who owns it? A ticket sent to the wrong team waits in the wrong queue.
      • 
          
    • Is it a repeat? Duplicate reports waste effort, and recurring failures point to a root cause that should be fixed once.
      • 
        
    
        
    The risk outcome is under-triage: a serious incident treated as minor. It delays the response, breaches resolution targets and lets customer-facing losses grow. Over-triage is cheaper but pulls senior engineers away from real outages. The project measures both with a cost matrix that weights under-triage of a SEV-1 incident 100 times more than a correct call.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Decision owner and scope
    
        
    
          
    
            
              
    DecisionSeverity, owning team and duplicate link for each new ticket
    
              
    Who decidesTriage coordinator on the service desk; the agent only suggests
    
              
    Risk taxonomyBasel II operational risk event types, mostly business disruption and system failures
    
              
    Harm measuredUnder-triage rate, SLA breaches, financial loss and customers affected
    
              
    Out of scopeFixing the incident, closing tickets and customer communication
    
                
          
    
        
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    
    
    
      Step 2 · Data sources, grain, time window and quality
      
    Two years of incident tickets, with the problems real ticket exports have
    
      
    The dataset is synthetic but built to behave like a real IT service management export. A hidden ground-truth file holds each ticket's true root cause, true severity and true duplicate link, so every method can be scored honestly.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Sources
    
        
      
          
    • Incident tickets: free text, timestamps, service, channel, team and severity
      • 
          
    • Resolution records: resolution time and notes
      • 
          
    • Loss events: customers affected and financial loss in USD
      • 
          
    • Change records: whether a change preceded the incident
      • 
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Grain and window
    
        
      
          
    • Grain: one row per incident ticket
      • 
          
    • Window:  to 
      • 
          
    • Training: , with Feb to Apr 2026 held back for model selection
      • 
          
    • Test:  ( tickets), never used for any choice
      • 
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Why a time-based split
    
        
    Random splits leak the future into training: the model would learn about an outage from tickets filed after it. Training on the past and testing on later months also exposes the model to real drift, including a new payments vendor that went live in July 2026.

      Step 3 · Analytical methods and trade-offs
      
    Simple, explainable models won on cost, and the LLM is kept as a measured challenger
    
      
    Each method was scored on the same untouched test months. The champion was chosen on the validation months by the lowest misclassification cost per ticket, not by accuracy alone, because under-triage is far more expensive than over-triage.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    Severity methodAccuracyMacro F1SEV-1 recallUnder-triageCost per ticketTrade-off
    
        
      
    
    
    
    
    Lower cost per ticket is better. Accuracy is measured against the severity staff recorded; against the hidden true severity the champion reaches , so part of the remaining error is noise in the recorded labels.
    

    
    
      
    
        
    Champion confusion matrix (test period)
    
        
    Rows are the severity staff recorded; columns are the model's suggestion. Cells below the diagonal are over-triage and cells above it are under-triage.
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Owner routing
    
        
    
        
    The owner model agrees with the recorded team  of the time, and with the team that should have owned it  of the time. Most disagreements are tickets staff sent to the wrong queue, which is the routing error the agent is meant to catch. In this synthetic data the owner follows directly from the root cause, so real-world accuracy will be lower.
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    LLM classifier
    
        
    The repository includes an LLM adapter with a strict JSON output contract, retries on schema errors and a fail-closed default. It handles unseen wording and gives a written reason, but costs money per ticket, adds latency and is harder to audit. It is scored on a sample of test tickets with scripts/run_llm_eval.py when an API key is available; no LLM numbers are shown here because none were run for this build.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Duplicate detection
    
        
    Each ticket is compared with earlier tickets on the same service within six hours. At the threshold chosen on validation, the agent flags duplicates with  precision and  recall. Staff marked  of true duplicates; of the  they missed, the agent found .
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Recurring-issue clustering
    
        
    Ticket text was reduced with latent semantic analysis (TF-IDF and truncated SVD) and grouped with k-means. The number of clusters, , was chosen by silhouette score. Against the hidden root causes the clusters score a normalized mutual information of .

      Step 4 · Agent workflow, tools, schemas and permission boundaries
      
    The agent can read and suggest. Only people can change a ticket.
    
      
    The agent runs a fixed sequence of tools for each new ticket and writes one structured suggestion. Every tool call, including refused ones, is written to an audit log.

      Step 5 · Validation, monitoring, human review and rollback
      
    Human review is designed around the cost of being wrong
    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    Review routing policy
    
        
      
          
    • Escalate now when the chance of SEV-1 is at least , whatever the top suggestion is.
      • 
          
    • Full review for any suggested SEV-1 or SEV-2, and whenever the model is unsure.
      • 
          
    • Fast path only when the chance of SEV-1 or SEV-2 is at most  and owner confidence is at least . A coordinator still confirms with one click.
      • 
        
    
        
    The fast-path cap was set on validation data as the largest value that kept high-severity incidents below 3% of the fast path.

      •

      •

    MonthTicketsSeverity overridesOwner overridesPSI, category mixPSI, predictionsFast pathStatus
    
    
    
    
    An override is a ticket where staff recorded a different value from the suggestion. Limits: overrides more than 3 points above the validation rate of  trigger a watch and more than 5 points an alert; PSI above 0.10 triggers a watch and above 0.25 an alert. The category-mix PSI crossed the watch limit in three of the four months after the NovaPay launch.
    

    
    
      
    
        
    Rollback
    
        
      
          
    • Kill switch in models/registry.json stops all suggestions; tickets fall back to manual triage at once.
      • 
          
    • Champion and fallback: if the champion fails monitoring, the registry points back to the keyword rules or the previous model version.
      • 
          
    • Versioned policy: routing thresholds are versioned separately from the model, so a policy can be rolled back without retraining.
      • 
          
    • No data to undo: the agent never writes to the system of record, so rolling back never requires repairing tickets.
      • 
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Triggers for retraining or rollback
    
        
      
          
    • Two consecutive months on alert
      • 
          
    • Any SEV-1 incident that reaches the fast path
      • 
          
    • Fast-path high-severity leakage above 3% in a month
      • 
          
    • A new vendor, service or team, such as the NovaPay integration

    Implementation plan
    
    
    
      
    Phase 1Weeks 1 to 3
    Data and baseline
    • Connect to the ticketing system read-only and build the cleaning pipeline
    • Agree the severity cost matrix with the service desk and operational risk
    • Train and back-test the models; document results for model risk review
    Gate: model risk sign-off on the validation report.
    
      
    Phase 2Weeks 4 to 7
    Shadow mode
    • The agent suggests on live tickets, but coordinators do not see the suggestions
    • Compare suggestions with human decisions every week
    • Tune the routing policy on live data
    Gate: no SEV-1 incident on the fast path for four weeks.
    
      
    Phase 3Weeks 8 to 11
    Assisted triage pilot
    • Show suggestions to one shift of coordinators, with accept and override buttons
    • Log every override as monitoring data and feedback for retraining
    • Measure time saved and coordinator trust
    Gate: success-metric targets met and coordinators support rollout.
    
      
    Phase 4Week 12 onward
    Rollout and run
    • Roll out to all shifts with the monthly monitoring report
    • Run the LLM challenger on hard tickets and compare cost and accuracy
    • Retrain quarterly, or sooner if monitoring triggers it
    Gate: quarterly model review with operational risk.
